A Blockcert project · free · open · work in progress

You have a digital footprint. You have never actually seen it.

Instagram knows what it showed you, when, how often, and what you did about it. It just never shows you. This turns your own data export into a dashboard you can read — themes, rhythm, attention, the accounts you keep watching without ever admitting it, and where the friction is.

Set up your own copy Read the code

Takes about 5 minutes. Needs a Google account and an Instagram data export.

Your data never leaves your Google account.

This is not a service and there is no server of mine to send anything to. You run your own private copy: the script lives in your Google Drive, reads the export sitting in your own folder, and writes to your own spreadsheet. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is sent to an AI, and I cannot see any of it — that is not a policy, it is just how the thing is built. The code is open, so you can check.

01Why bother

The mission is narrow and worth saying plainly: higher consciousness about our digital footprints, and better digital well-being. Not a detox app, not a lecture about screen time. Evidence about your own behaviour, in your hands, that nobody else gets a copy of.

The feed is not neutral

What you see is chosen for you. Once it is laid out by theme and hour, you can see the shape of that choice — and how much of your attention went somewhere you never picked.

Likes are performance

Likes are what you are willing to show. Repeated viewing is what actually held you. The gap between those two is the most honest thing in the whole export.

You cannot change what you cannot see

Late-night drift, a narrowing feed, an emotional tone creeping one way. These are measurable, and measurable things can be answered.

It is your data

Meta must hand it over when you ask. Almost nobody does, because the export is a pile of unreadable HTML. This makes it readable — and keeps it yours.

02What you get

A spreadsheet with about twenty tabs, and a private dashboard that reads it. Every number is rule-based and every rule is visible and editable — nothing here is a judgement about you.

Themes

What the feed actually fed you, by share, with subtopics and month-on-month movement.

Rhythm

Hour by hour and weekday by weekday. Estimated minutes, sessions, late-night share.

Quiet interests

Accounts you kept seeing and never once reacted to — attention you give but do not express.

Network

Everyone you follow, who follows you, and who the feed keeps pushing, drawn as a graph.

Belonging

Seen, Heard, Invested in, Connected — scored where the data allows, and marked unmeasurable where it does not.

Risk signals

Eleven behavioural indicators scored likelihood × impact, with what to try for each.

These are behavioural indicators built from keyword rules over what you were shown. They are not a clinical assessment, not a security audit, and not a verdict on you. The formulas are printed next to the numbers so you can disagree with them.

03Set it up

Two things happen once, then it runs itself. If you get stuck at any step, open an issue — a confusing step is a bug in this page, not in you.

  1. Ask Instagram for your data

    Do this first — Meta can take a few hours to a day to send the first one.

    The menu names below are the Italian ones, because that is what the screenshots show. If your Instagram is in English the path is identical: Accounts Centre → Your information and permissions → Export your information.

    1. Open Instagram and tap the hamburger menu (☰) at the top right, then Impostazioni e attività.
    2. Tap Centro gestione account.
    3. Tap Le tue informazioni e autorizzazioni.
    4. Tap Esporta le tue informazioni.
    5. Tap Crea esportazione, then choose the profile you want to export.
    6. Choose Esporta su un servizio esterno.
    7. Choose Google Drive.
    8. Pick Weekly, and how long it should keep running — at least 1 year. Then tap Collega and grant access to your Google Drive. It may ask for your Google password.
    9. On the last screen, set all four: Personalizza informazioni → everything selected · Intervallo di date → Ultima settimana · Formato → HTML · Qualità dei contenuti multimediali → Qualità inferiore. Then Avvia esportazione.

    1 / 8
    Why weekly, and why HTML. Instagram only keeps about a week of viewing history in any one export, so a weekly schedule straight into Drive is how you build a real record over time instead of a handful of snapshots. JSON exports are skipped, and the media is never needed — low quality keeps the files small.
    English and Italian exports both work. Other languages will parse partially — the dates and field labels are what have been checked so far.

  2. Make your own copy of the sheet

    One click. You get a private copy in your own Drive, with the script already inside it — no code to paste.

    Make a copy →

    Prefer to read the code before running it? Every line is on GitHub, and the README has the manual paste-it-yourself route.

  3. Run setup once

    In your new sheet: Extensions → Apps Script, pick setup from the function dropdown, and press Run.

    Google will ask for permission to use your Drive, Sheets and triggers, and will warn you the app is unverified. That warning is expected: it is your copy of the script, running as you, and Google has not reviewed it because it was never submitted for review. Choose Advanced → Go to (project name) to continue.

    Setup creates an Instagram Exports folder in your Drive and links it. It also prints the folder link so you can find it.

  4. Drop the export in

    When Meta's email arrives, put the .zip straight into that Instagram Exports folder — unzipping is optional, and the Drive app on your phone works fine.

    Then reload the sheet and choose Instagram Insights → Process new exports now. After that it checks by itself once a day, so every month you only have to drop the new file in.

  5. Open your dashboard

    The sheet is already readable, but the dashboard is nicer. In the Apps Script editor: Deploy → New deployment → Web app, then set Execute as: Me and Who has access: Only myself.

    Keep it on "Only myself." That deployment runs as you and reads your spreadsheet, so anyone you gave the link to would be reading your Instagram history through your own account. This is also why there is no shared dashboard to hand you — the only safe version is the one that belongs to you.

04Where this is going

Work in progress

This is unfinished and openly so. It starts at Instagram because that export is the richest one Meta actually hands over — but Instagram is the starting point, not the point. Anything that leaves a digital footprint you are entitled to read could be pulled into the same frame, always under the same rule: your data stays in your account.

  • Working
    InstagramThemes, rhythm, quiet interests, network graph, belonging, risk register.
  • Next
    Belonging, properlyTwo of its four dimensions cannot be measured from an Instagram export at all. The dashboard says so out loud instead of faking a score — closing that gap needs better sources.
  • Next
    Outward, not just inwardThe dashboard describes consumption. The first feature that points outward — real places and groups near you, by interest — is designed and not yet built.
  • Later
    Other footprintsOther platforms that offer real data exports, and a way to read several of them side by side.
  • Later
    Longer memoryMost of what matters only becomes visible across a year, not a week. That needs time and exports, not code.

Requests and disagreements are genuinely useful here — especially "this number is wrong and here is why". The rules behind every metric are editable in the Settings tab of your own copy, so you can also just fix it for yourself.

Free, and staying free

There is nothing to buy, no account to make, no upsell and no data of yours to sell — there could not be, since none of it ever reaches me. If it was useful and you feel like it, you can put something in the tin. Entirely optional, and it changes nothing about what you get.
